Written for the people the advice forgets.

Most sleep guidance assumes you choose your bedtime. SleepMedic is for everyone else: 24s and 48s, three twelves in a row, rotating nights, and the months after a baby arrives.

Why this exists

Go to bed at ten. Wake at six. No caffeine after two. That advice is fine, and it is useless if your schedule rotates every few weeks, if you are driving home as the sun comes up, or if going to bed earlier is simply not something you are allowed to do.

The underlying biology does not change between a day worker and a night worker. Circadian timing, sleep pressure, light, core temperature, arousal: same mechanisms, same research. What changes is which levers you can actually reach. If you cannot control when you sleep, you can still control light exposure, caffeine timing, wind-down, and how you recover between shifts. That is the subject here.

Shift workers and first responders get the most attention on this site because they are the hardest case. Protocols that hold up across a 48 hold up for everyone else too.

How we source

The value of this site rests entirely on whether the citations hold up. So:

  • Primary firstPeer-reviewed journals, CDC data, AASM guidelines, and NIH publications before secondary coverage. Secondary sources are used only where they represent the underlying research accurately.
  • Cited inlineEvery substantive claim links to the study or the authoritative summary it came from, so you can check the work rather than take our word for it.
  • Disagreement statedWhere the evidence is mixed, thin, or context-dependent, we say so instead of smoothing it into a confident recommendation. Where a popular claim conflicts with the primary literature, we say that too.
  • No paid placementNo sponsored content and no affiliate arrangements that influence what gets covered or recommended. If a commercial relationship ever exists, it gets disclosed in the post.
  • Corrections keptWhen something here turns out to be wrong, it gets fixed in place and the change is noted rather than quietly deleted.

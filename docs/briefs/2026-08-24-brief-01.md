Subject: Your two days off are not the same day

---

First brief. Here's the format, every week: one protocol, one study, one
field note. Short enough to read in the bay.

**The protocol.** If you work a 24 or a string of nights, the day you come
off is not a day off — it's a recovery day, and it has different rules. A
shorter sleep held near your work-day wake time, one planned nap, an early
night. The full sleep-in belongs to the day after, on your rest-day anchor.
Chasing eight hours the morning you come off is what steals the next night.
The complete version, walked end to end, is in the new guides:
[Sleep on the 24/48](https://www.sleepmedic.co/guides/24-48/) and
[Sleep on Nights, 3x12](https://www.sleepmedic.co/guides/nights-3x12/).

**The study.** In 2006, researchers at Harvard tested people's thinking at
the moment they woke from a full night of sleep. It was worse than after a
night of total sleep deprivation ([Wertz et al., JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16403927/)).
The fog burns off in about half an hour, but you don't get a vote on when
the tones drop. That's why the on-call nap is 20 to 30 minutes: you wake
from shallow sleep and skip the deep end of the fog. The full nap logic is
in [The Nap Menu](https://www.sleepmedic.co/blog/posts/2026-08-22-the-nap-menu.html).

**The field note.** I put my alarm somewhere high, across the room. The
only way to silence it is to get out of bed, and the rule is you don't get
back in. No trial behind that one. It works anyway.
